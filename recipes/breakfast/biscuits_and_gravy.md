2 cups flour  
1 tablespoon sugar  
2 teaspoons baking powder  
1 teaspoon salt  
1/2 teaspoon baking soda  
1 cup butter  
3/4 cup buttermilk

At least double it and roll it thicker than you think

Speaking of liquid, I used to use powdered buttermilk, but have started just souring my own milk. Just measure the milk first, stir in a tablespoon of lemon juice, and let it sit while you prepare the other ingredients. (It's the acid that reacts with the baking powder to make them rise.)

Preheat oven to 425º. Mix all dry ingredients together. Cut in butter with pastry blender (there should still be chunks). Stir in buttermilk until just combined. Turn dough onto a floured board, pat into a rectangle. Fold the rectangle in thirds and flatten back into a rectangle. Repeat twice more, folding and pressing dough a total of three times. Roll dough to about 1/2 inch thick. Cut out biscuits using whatever round thing you have. Bake on greased cookie sheet for about 15 minutes.

You probably don't have a pastry blender. Some recipes will tell you to use knives or forks to cut up the butter in the flour, but it either won't be good enough or will take forever. This alone would be reason enough to just use canned biscuits rather than make them from scratch. Believe me, if you're cooking for just one or two, it's just not worth it. If you're set on it, go to Walmart and drop \$8 on a pastry blender.  
The amount of liquid and the number of times you fold and press will determine how much your biscuits rise. This will be a trial and error process until you find what works for you.  
Speaking of liquid, I used to use powdered buttermilk, but have started just souring my own milk. Just measure the milk first, stir in a tablespoon of lemon juice, and let it sit while you prepare the other ingredients. (It's the acid that reacts with the baking powder to make them rise.)  
Also, if you don't have cookie/biscuit cutters, give some thought to what you use. The sharper the edge the better, so it doesn't just pinch. It's also better if it's not a small, closed container. A small cup, for example, will just smash the dough because of the air pressure inside it. A large one means less volume differential. If you have a plastic cup you can cut a hole into the bottom of, even better. Or just buy canned biscuits...

### Sausage Gravy {#sausage-gravy}

1 pound breakfast sausage  
1/4 cup flour  
2 cups milk  
1/2 teaspoon salt  
Pepper to taste

Try doubling the fluid

Brown sausage in skillet. Add flour and stir until absorbed. Add milk, salt, and pepper and bring to a boil, stirring (and scraping the bottom of the pan) constantly. Remove from heat as soon as it's boiling.

The gravy is basically a white sauce with sausage mixed in. Usually you'd make it with equal parts melted butter and flour, but I just don't drain the sausage and use the rendered grease as the base. If your sausage is especially lean, you'll need to melt butter into it before adding the flour.  
Obviously if your gravy's too thick, just add a little more milk.

One other thing that occurred to me as I was making these yesterday. If you preheat the milk, you don't have to spend as long stirring and there's less risk of the gravy burning in the pan. Just throw it in the microwave in the measuring cup or a bowl while you're cooking the sausage so it will be hot when you're ready to add it. Most low-density \`plastics are not made to withstand actual boiling temperatures, though, so use glass. Happy cooking\!
