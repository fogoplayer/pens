## Grandma Loosli’s Coffee Cake (From _Apron Strings_) {#grandma-loosli’s-coffee-cake-(from-apron-strings)}

From the Loosli family

| 3 cups flour (we use 2 white and 1 wheat) 2 cups brown sugar 1 tsp. cinnamon ½ tsp. salt | ½ cup butter or margarine 2 eggs ⅔ cup milk\* 2 tsp baking powder |
| :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------- |

\*I’ve always thought this was actually a transcription error in doubling the original recipe. You can make it this way, but it will be very dense. If you double the milk, it will be more cakey.

Preheat oven to 350 degrees. Mix first four ingredients, and cut in butter with a pastry blender until crumbly. Set aside ¾ cup of mixture for topping. Mix eggs, milk, and baking powder with remaining mixture and pour into lightly-greased 9 x 13 pan. Sprinkle topping over batter and bake 25-30 minutes.

Kyle Loosli says:  
_“We have a tattered piece of notebook paper with this recipe written in grandma’s own hand, and we make it at least twice a month. I love the darkness of the cake from the brown sugar and the powdery topping; I remember picking off the little nuggets of caramelized brown sugar when no one was looking, a tradition that our kids have enthusiastically embraced. Growing up, this also was my version of bread and milk (or cold cereal, for you pampered young ’uns): put a piece in a bowl, pour milk over it, cut or mash it up, and enjoy. I don’t do that anymore, but I should probably try it out on my dunk-happy kids.”_

Charisse Thomas says:  
_“Yummy stuff\! My biggest memory of coffee cake is (besides not understanding at first how we could eat coffee cake but not drink coffee) was timing how quickly you ate your piece based on how far along in the pan the servings had gotten. Since dad insisted we go one side to the other from one end to the other (no jumping ahead) I would either nibble slowly so as to miss the edges being dished out, or inhale (not an easy feat considering the dusty topping) so I could snag a coveted middle piece. They were heaven. Oh, and licking the plate, table and anything else the sugary goodness from the top had fallen on.”_
