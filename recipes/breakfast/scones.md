## Scones {#scones}

We had scones recently, and I was surprised at the reminder of how much the kids like them. That may be the one thing that they don't eat less of now that you're gone. I assume you don't have a bread machine, but when me and mom first got married, we used to buy frozen bread dough and keep it on hand to do different things with. The key is to make sure it not only thaws completely, but actually gets warm enough to rise before you cook it. You can thaw it in the fridge, at least 12 but preferably 24 hours, then leave it on the counter for 2-3 hours or in a warm oven (turn it on for a few minutes, then turn it off and put the dough in and keep the door closed) for 30-60 minutes. This is not fast food. It will warm faster if you flatten it somewhat while it's still cold. Anyway, once it's warm, roll it out (you can use a can like non-stick spray if you don't have a rolling pin), cut it into squares or rhombi, and fry in an inch or so of oil over medium high heat. Make sure the oil is hot enough to pop if you flick a drop of water into it before you start cooking. Top with butter, honey, jam, nutella, frosting, whatever. You know what to do from there.

Or, just buy refrigerator biscuits and deep fry them plain or press them and fold them in half around some chocolate chips. The key here is powdered sugar. Wasn't that easier? We'll have scones sometime when you get home.
